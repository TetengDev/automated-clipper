## Editing playbook: rules for every clip

These rules apply to every creator. The content-type section that follows (if any) adds
to them; it never overrides the sensitive-moment rules below.

- **Vertical, fast, self-contained.** A clip is watched on a phone, muted at first, by
  someone who has never seen the channel. The first line has to make sense on its own and
  the on-screen hook card has to say what is at stake.
- **One idea per clip.** Cut away side topics. If the moment needs two ideas to land, it is
  probably two clips or none.
- **Earn the ending.** The payoff (the reveal, the punchline, the answer, the reaction)
  must land inside the clip; the clip ends on it or shortly after it, never before it.
  Never end on a cliffhanger the clip itself sets up and then leaves unanswered.
- **Captions in the speaker's language.** Keep the words the speaker used, including
  Tagalog and Taglish; do not translate or tidy up slang.
- **Effects follow the content.** Zooms, sound effects, emoji and meme inserts are only
  proposed where the footage already has the energy for them. When unsure, propose less.

### Titles, captions and hashtags per platform

Every candidate carries `variants`: one title, caption and hashtag list each for TikTok,
Instagram, YouTube and Facebook.

- **Lead with the keyword a viewer would search.** The title and the first caption line
  start with the name, object or event people type into search (for example "MrBeast
  island giveaway"), never with filler like "Wait for it" or "This is crazy".
- **The hook line comes first.** The first 60 characters of every caption state what the
  clip is about; platforms cut the rest off behind "more".
- **3 to 5 hashtags, specific first.** The most specific tag (the person, show or event)
  goes first, then the topic. No generic reach tags: never #fyp, #foryou, #foryoupage,
  #viral, #trending or #fy. Instagram allows at most 5.
- **Within each platform's limits.** Titles at most 100 characters (YouTube shows about
  70). Captions at most 2,200 characters on TikTok, Instagram and Facebook, and the YouTube
  description at most 5,000; short is better on all four. TikTok, Instagram and Facebook
  may reuse the title as the first caption line.
- Campaign and sponsor tags are added later by the scheduler; never add them yourself.
- The sensitive-moment rules below apply to every variant: no protected person's or
  private complainant's identifiers in any title, caption or hashtag.

### Sensitive moments

A moment is sensitive when it involves a victim of a crime or accident, a minor in
distress, death, serious illness, abuse, a tragedy or disaster, or someone's private
hardship (debt, family conflict, a medical or legal problem).

- Set `sensitive` to true for these moments.
- No sound effects, meme inserts, emoji pops or comedic zooms over a victim, a minor, or
  a tragedy. Plain cuts and plain captions only.
- The hook and title describe what happened in a neutral way. Never make a person's
  suffering the joke, and never sensationalise it beyond what was said.
- Protected people are a victim in any abuse, sexual (including rape), family or child
  case, and any minor. "Family" includes spouses, partners and unpaid support
  (sustento). Never choose a moment in which anyone, at any point, says something that
  identifies them: the subtitles show every spoken word, so a role in the hook does not
  hide it. That covers anything that, alone or together, tends to identify them: their
  name or nickname; the name of a parent, spouse, partner or relative; the name of the
  respondent in an abuse, sexual, family/support or child case; their school,
  workplace, employer, barangay, town, address, phone or plate number; or a relationship
  combined with a place. Also never choose a moment in which the transcript shows they
  are on camera (they are called forward, introduced, or speak). Faces are checked by
  the reviewer, who sees the video.
- Never put the name, school, workplace, address or phone number of a private
  complainant in any other case in the hook, title, caption or hashtags, and never
  choose a start whose first spoken line names them. Refer to people by role (for
  example "a complainant", "a 15-year-old"). This overrides "the hook names the person".
- Do not choose moments whose subtitles, hook, title or caption would reveal a private
  person's address or phone number.
- If a moment cannot be clipped respectfully, leave it out.
